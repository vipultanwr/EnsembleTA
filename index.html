Project EnsembleTA: A Post-Mortem and Forward Look

By: Vipul Tanwar | November 25, 2025 | GitHub Project

Executive Summary

Project EnsembleTA was an initiative to develop a quantitative trading strategy by combining a large number of technical indicators. After a rigorous backtesting process, the results conclusively demonstrate that the current strategy is not profitable and carries a high risk of significant capital loss. This document serves as a post-mortem of the initial strategy and outlines a clear, data-driven path for future development. The findings, though negative, showcase a commitment to rigorous analysis, intellectual honesty, and an iterative, scientific approach to strategy development.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

The goal of Project EnsembleTA was to test the hypothesis that a "wisdom of the crowds" approach could be applied to technical indicators. The idea was to build a framework that could:

  1. Systematically evaluate hundreds of technical indicators from the TA-Lib library.
  2. Rank these indicators based on their historical performance.
  3. Create an ensemble "voting" signal from the top-performing indicators.
  4. Backtest the final ensemble strategy on out-of-sample data to validate its performance.

2. Methodology & Technology

The project was developed in Python and leveraged a standard quantitative finance stack:

  • Data Acquisition: yfinance and ccxt for historical price data (OHLCV).
  • Data Analysis: pandas for data manipulation and numpy for numerical operations.
  • Indicator Generation: TA-Lib for generating over 100 technical indicators.
  • Backtesting: A custom-built, vectorized backtesting engine.
  • Orchestration: Scripts to automate running hundreds of backtests with different hyperparameters.

3. Backtesting & The Reality Check: A Flawed Strategy

The largeSims1 branch was created to run a comprehensive set of backtests across a diverse range of assets (crypto, stocks, forex, commodities) and timeframes. The orchestrator script ran 450 unique backtests.

Key Findings:

  • Overwhelmingly Negative Returns: The vast majority of tests resulted in significant losses, with total returns often between -50% and -90%.
  • Consistently Negative Sharpe Ratios: The risk-adjusted returns were consistently negative, indicating the strategy failed to compensate for the risk it took.
  • A Single, Misleading Outlier: Only one test out of 450 showed a minor positive return (5.13% on GOOG, 1wk), which was traced back to a single lucky trade rather than a robust strategic edge.

4. Root Cause Analysis

  1. Lack of Effective Signal Generation

    The strategy's core assumption—that a simple majority vote from over 100 indicators with generic, hardcoded thresholds would produce a meaningful signal—was incorrect. This "one-size-fits-all" approach failed to adapt to the unique characteristics of different assets and market regimes. The result was a final signal with no discernible predictive power.

  2. Absence of Risk Management

    The backtesting engine and the strategy itself lacked any form of risk management. There were no stop-loss orders to cap losses on individual trades, nor were there take-profit orders to secure gains. This is a critical omission in any trading strategy, as it allows a few losing trades to wipe out any potential profits and lead to catastrophic drawdowns.

5. Recommendations & Future Work

The failure of the initial strategy is a valuable learning experience. The following steps are recommended to pivot the project towards a more promising direction. This demonstrates an ability to learn from failure and iterate—a critical skill in quantitative research.

  1. Abandon the Current Ensemble Method: The next iteration should focus on a small number of well-understood, configurable indicators (e.g., RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands).
  2. Prioritize Risk Management: The immediate next step is to implement stop-loss and take-profit functionality within the backtesting engine.
  3. Adopt a "Simple First" Approach: Start with a classic, simple strategy (e.g., a dual moving average crossover) and ensure it can be backtested and validated correctly.
  4. Develop a More Sophisticated Signal Generation Process: Explore machine learning models (e.g., logistic regression, gradient boosting) to combine signals in a more intelligent way.

While Project EnsembleTA did not produce a profitable trading strategy in its first iteration, it was a success in building a robust backtesting framework and in demonstrating a rigorous, analytical process. The project's value lies not in the initial, flawed strategy, but in the clear, data-driven conclusions drawn from its failure and the well-defined path for future development.
